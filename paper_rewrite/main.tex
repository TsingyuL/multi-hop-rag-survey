\begin{abstract}
Multi-hop retrieval and reasoning addresses information needs whose solution requires a sequence of dependent acquisitions: evidence found at one step determines what can be searched, verified, or combined next. Unlike single-shot retrieval-augmented generation, a multi-hop system must maintain an evolving state, formulate later information needs, control search depth, and integrate evidence collected across steps. These requirements now arise in multi-hop question answering, graph retrieval, adaptive retrieval-augmented generation, agentic search, deep research, and systems with persistent memory or reusable skills.

We present a functional survey organized around four stages: \emph{initial retrieval}, which obtains entry evidence from the original task; \emph{follow-up retrieval}, which acquires later evidence conditioned on intermediate results; \emph{evidence integration}, which selects, preserves, organizes, reasons over, and synthesizes the acquired sources; and \emph{feedback learning}, which uses completed trajectories to improve future retrieval and reasoning. Deep search and deep research are treated as system-level compositions of these functions rather than separate top-level categories. The review connects early multi-hop question answering and path retrieval with modern graph-based, long-context, browser-based, reinforcement-learning, memory, and skill-learning systems. Its companion artifact contains a \CorpusSize-record literature corpus and a stratified \AuditSize-paper full-text audit selection, while exposing record-level verification status.

We further develop a claim-aligned evaluation framework covering entry recall, hop completion, search trajectories, stopping, evidence preservation and use, grounding, efficiency, and cross-task improvement. The survey therefore frames multi-hop reasoning not as longer generation, but as adaptive information seeking: identifying what is known, determining what is missing, deciding what to search next, integrating the resulting evidence, and learning from prior attempts.
\end{abstract}
